Playground prices · cost guide How much does a playground cost?

There is no single price for a playground, because no two sites are the same. This guide explains what drives the cost, so you can plan a realistic budget before you ask for a quotation.

1. The equipment itself

The largest part of most budgets is the play equipment: how many units, how big they are and how many children they serve at once. A single tower with a slide and a landmark play landscape for a compound are very different projects.

Materials matter too. Wood, steel, HDPE panels and fibreglass each have a different cost, look and lifespan. Custom designs and theming cost more than standard catalogue units.

2. Safety surfacing

Every unit needs impact-absorbing surfacing across its safety zone, and that zone is often several times larger than the equipment. Rubber tiles, poured rubber, artificial grass with shock pads and sand all cost differently, so surfacing can be a large share of the total.

3. Site works and shade

Levelling, drainage, foundations, edging, paths and access all add to the budget. In Egypt's climate, shade is not optional for daytime use: shade sails or structures should be planned from the start.

4. Installation and location

Installation depends on the size of the equipment, access to the site and its location. A coastal resort or a remote site needs more logistics than a city school yard.

5. How to get an accurate quotation

Send a site plan or photos with rough measurements, the age groups and number of children, the type of space and your target opening date. With those, a supplier can propose a layout that fits your space and price it properly instead of guessing.

Key takeaways

  • Equipment, surfacing, site works and installation are the four main cost drivers.
  • Safety zones decide how much surfacing you need, so plan them first.
  • Plan shade from the start in Egypt's climate.
  • A site plan and age groups are enough to start a quotation.
